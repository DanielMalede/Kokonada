1What is being asked

Permission to read a defined set of measurements from {{sourceName}}, and to use them to choose music for you. Nothing else. Consent is separate from your account: you can hold an account, refuse this permanently, and the app still works.

2Exactly what is read
• Heart rate
• Heart-rate variability (HRV)
• Sleep sessions
• Resting heart rate

Those four are the data types. History is a separate permission on top of them: the one we ask for covers your whole stored history, and we choose to read back about six months and no further.

Agreeing here is not the same as granting the operating system's permission. Straight after this, {{sheetPhrase}} and asks separately. You can refuse there, and you can revoke it later in {{sourceName}} without coming back here.

3What it is used for

One purpose: computing how activated and how settled your body is right now, and turning that into musical targets — a tempo range, an energy ceiling, how acoustic the set should be. It also learns your personal baseline, so "normal" means normal for you rather than for a population average. The reading is done by an automated system, not by a person.

4Who else processes it

Companies that process data on our behalf: Groq (United States) generates the music intelligence; Railway (United States) hosts the service; MongoDB Atlas (REGION) stores the database; Sentry (REGION) receives error reports, which can include your account ID. The current, complete list is always in the privacy notice. Where a processor sits outside Europe, the transfer runs on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses; ask and we will send you a copy. Groq receives derived, non-identifying signals — an abstract mood coordinate, a coarse intensity band, closed-vocabulary intent tags, and mood-descriptor genre tags — used both to choose tracks and to estimate how a track feels. Once, when you first connect YouTube, it also receives the artist names in your library, to build your music profile. It never receives a heart rate, a sleep record, your name, your email, or anything from Spotify. Groq returns the musical targets; we apply them here. Music playback runs through the provider you connect, which sees what it plays, not why.

5Storage and encryption

Readings are encrypted in transit, and encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM on our servers. They are never written to application logs. Your raw readings — the actual heart rate, the actual sleep record — are never sent to an AI provider; what is sent is the coarse band described in section 4.

6How long it is kept

Readings are kept for 90 days and then deleted automatically — including one live reading a minute while a session is open, which is saved so your baseline stays accurate. The body profile learned from them — your resting heart rate, HRV and sleep baselines — has no fixed expiry: it is kept while this consent is on. What the app learned about your music expires after a year with no listening. Withdrawing deletes both, and deleting your account deletes everything. Derived states and the sets they produced stay with your history until you delete them. The record of this consent is kept until you delete your account, because it is the proof you were asked.

7Your rights

You may withdraw this consent at any time in Profile, without giving a reason and without losing your account. Withdrawal stops all reading immediately and deletes the readings already held, including the physiological baseline learned from them and the taste profile the app built while it was on — that is not recoverable, and rebuilding it means starting from scratch. It does not affect the lawfulness of what was processed before. You may also ask for a copy of everything held about you, receive it in a portable machine-readable file, ask for it to be corrected or erased, ask us to restrict how we use it, and lodge a complaint with your national supervisory authority.

8Who is responsible

Kokonada is the data controller. Written requests reach a person, not a form: PRIVACY CONTACT. A response is due within one month and is usually much faster.
