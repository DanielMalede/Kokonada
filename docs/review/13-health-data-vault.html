The withdrawal and deletion surface. Three treatments, both themes, seven independent rows. You asked for a considered answer on whether the mini-player belongs here; that is below, and it turned out to have an evidence trail rather than only a tone.

The warning for the irreversible action is the one thing below the fold

Measured, in the scroll region: the “Delete my account” button ends 12px above the fold — visible. Its explanation, “This one cannot be undone. Everything goes — your account, your history, and every reading we hold”, runs from y=676 to y=709 in a 681px window. 28 of its 33 pixels are below the fold. Roughly five pixels of the sentence are on screen.

So the screen shows a red irreversible button with its warning cut off, and there are only 28px of scroll left to reveal it — barely enough travel to suggest there is anything down there. Of everything on this screen, the sentence that explains what cannot be undone is the part the layout hides.

Your standing question: does the player belong here?

You asked for a considered answer rather than a default. Mine is no, and not only on tone.

It occludes the warning. The player bar sits directly beneath the fold that is cutting the delete explanation in half. Whatever else is true, a transport bar is taking the space where the consequence of an irreversible action should be.

It is a retention affordance at the foot of a withdrawal screen. That is structurally the same family as making Agree louder than Decline: the surface where someone is deciding to leave is not the surface on which to keep the product playing. I do not think it was intended that way, and I think it reads that way.

The third-party mark makes a claim it should not. Spotify has nothing to do with health-data consent. Its presence at the foot of this screen implies a relationship between the music provider and the health readings, on the one screen where a user is most alert to exactly that question — and the copy immediately above it is busy promising “never an AI provider, never an advertiser”.

The consistency objection does not hold, because this is not a tab. The board draws a five-item tab bar with PROFILE selected and no back affordance — so it presents as a tab-level screen while having no tab of its own, and (as screen 12 found) nothing anywhere navigates to it. §7b already set the precedent for this exact case: the Mix receipt is pushed, carries a back affordance and no tab bar. The Vault is the same kind of screen. Make it pushed, and the player and the tab bar both stop being questions.

Correcting your note on the destructive token

You wrote that “Delete my account” is red and “no red or destructive token is declared in the system”. Half of that is not so: danger is declared — #9E3346 light and #FF9BA8 dark in tokens.ts, and now in the canvas source layer too. It measures 6.96:1 and 8.64:1 on the raised surface, so it clears the text floor comfortably.

What genuinely does not exist is a token for destructive actions as distinct from errors — grep -rn "destructive" over the mobile source returns nothing. But the convention already exists in the code and is written down: ProfileScreen.tsx:358 uses state.danger for account deletion, and VaultConsentPanel.tsx:14 states the rule outright — “NEUTRAL brand (accent.glowInk), never state.danger — that hue is reserved for account deletion.” So declaring the token codifies a practice the codebase already follows rather than inventing a distinction. You already ruled that error colour is a different axis from the emotion accent (§7.2) and explicitly left this case open for this screen. Row two is that decision, and it is worth noting the two uses have opposite intents: an error says something went wrong; a destructive action says you are about to do something deliberate and final.

What else I measured

“Withdraw health consent” is the undeclared blue — #3A5CCC, painted ten times across it and “Read what I agreed to”. The shipped component is explicit about what it should be: VaultConsentPanel.tsx states “Withdrawal is a RIGHT, not destruction: NEUTRAL brand (accent.glowInk), never state.danger”. So the intent is already written down and the board is using the wrong token to express it — --brandink measures 6.62:1 / 5.34:1.

“Version 1 · agreed [DATE]” is not half-fillable. It is entirely unfillable, and I had this wrong. I wrote that the version was derivable and only the date was missing. A verification pass refuted it: currentVersion is the server’s CURRENT_CONSENT_VERSION constant — the version the terms are at now, not the version the user agreed to. The granted version exists only server-internally via getGrantedConsentVersion (consent.js:110-113), which no route exposes. No timestamp is sent either: grantedAt and withdrawnAt live on the server-side ConsentRecord and are absent from getConsentStatus’s return, the sole payload of all three consent endpoints.

So the client can truthfully say exactly three things: a grant is on file, what version the terms are currently at, and whether the grant on file is behind that. The shipped panel is honest about this — VaultConsentPanel.tsx:59 renders only “Consent on file — personalising with your body’s signals.” and shows no date or version at all. The date, the granted version and any prior withdrawal are all real data the server holds; all three need exposing before this line can be drawn.
