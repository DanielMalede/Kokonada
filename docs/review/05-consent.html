Three treatments, both themes, seven independent rows, live assembly at the top. This is the Article 9(2)(a) surface, so most of what follows is about whether the screen tells the truth, not how it looks.

What I found on this screen

9.3% of the notice is on screen at once. You said the scroll area was “very short”. Measured, the notice box renders 174px tall and its content is 1870px. The reader is looking at the legal notice through a window about a paragraph and a half deep, and the board draws no scrollbar, no fade, and no affordance saying there is more. On a consent surface, “the information was available” and “the information was presented” are different claims, and 174px of 1870px is closer to the first.

Two accent surfaces on a screen the spec requires to be inert. The shield glyph strokes var(--accent) and “Read the full privacy notice” is the same colour — three painted instances, #3A5CCC light and #9DB4FF dark. Worth being precise about the reason: both pass contrast (5.63:1 and 9.46:1). The objection is the rule, not the ratio.

The board discloses a data type the shipped code does not collect. Its “also covered” row names Daily readiness. GARMIN_ONLY_DATA_CATEGORIES is exactly ['spo2', 'respiratory_rate', 'body_battery'] — three items. Daily readiness is added on the unmerged fix/daily-readiness-consent-gate branch. So the canvas is ahead of the code on the one screen where the list is the contract. Whichever way it resolves, the notice and CONSENT_DATA_CATEGORIES have to say the same thing on the same day.

The retention paragraph is wrong about the baseline, in the generous direction. §6 says the personal baseline is “kept for as long as your account exists”. PersonalWeights.js:99 puts a 365-day TTL keyed to updatedAt on it — an active listener never expires, but 365 days of silence deletes it. Readings are 90 days (BiometricLog.js:43), and so is MorningState. Article 13(2)(a) wants the period or the criteria; “as long as your account exists” is neither, and it is not what the code does. Note also that all three are process.env-overridable, so the stated period is a deployment promise, not a code guarantee.

The shipped screen is worse than the board here, and should be fixed with it. ConsentSheet.tsx’s retention section reads “Raw samples are minimised over time” — no period at all, which is a straight Article 13(2)(a) failure. That file also carries an explicit banner: “PLACEHOLDER COPY — final legal wording pending compliance-auditor + legal review.” Nothing here should be read as settled legal text, and I have not treated it as such.

The labels that define what you are consenting to are declared twice. DATA_TYPE_LABELS exists in ConsentSheet.tsx:19 and again in VaultConsentPanel.tsx:22. Same problem you just had me fix for colour, applied to the strings a user reads when granting and when withdrawing — two files that can disagree about what was consented to. The categories themselves are correctly derived from one constant; only the human-readable labels are duplicated.

What I want your eye on

Decline and Agree are drawn identically — same border, same weight, same colour. On a consent screen that is defensible and probably correct: making Agree louder is the classic dark pattern, and the spec says no dark patterns. But identical also means there is no primary at all, and the only thing distinguishing the irreversible choice from the reversible one is the word. The Actions row gives you three readings of that, including one where Agree is filled — shown so you can reject it deliberately rather than by omission.

The real fork is the notice itself. A 1870px legal document does not belong in a 174px window, but the alternatives all cost something: a full-screen notice means the user leaves this screen to read it, and accordion sections mean the default state is collapsed — which is its own kind of not-presented. That is row three, and I do not think there is a free answer.
