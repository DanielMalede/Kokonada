The copy contradicts a requirement. Panel 1's supporting line reads "Up to three places. Nothing to pick from a list." The app is required to ship a text/list alternative to the plate for screen-reader and motor-impairment users — UI_UX_OVERHAUL_SPEC.md §7, and it exists on Generate as the "Words" control. The onboarding tells every user the opposite of what the accessible path offers. The "Supporting line" row below resolves it.

The screen whose job is to teach the map shows an unlabelled map. The plate is drawn with its two dashed axes and no axis labels, so it teaches that there is a disc, not what the disc means. Every variant here labels the axes.

It is one panel of three. Panels 2 (the body line) and 3 (the rising arc) were never drawn. Variant A includes all three.

Carried from screen 01, and what I want your eye on

Your Splash choice constrains this screen. You picked wave alone with mark travels, so the element arriving here is the wave — not the ring-and-wave lockup. Every variant below receives it at the top and lets it settle before the content moves. If a variant here has nowhere for the wave to land, that's a defect in the variant.

The real question is whether this screen should exist at all. SCREENS.md §2 wants it "cinematic, wordless where possible… the value-prop moment before any permission ask." Variant C takes that literally and replaces explanation with one real gesture. It is the strongest and the riskiest: it teaches the mechanic and the promise in a single touch, but it puts an interactive surface in front of someone who has not agreed to anything yet, and a throwaway read has to be visibly throwaway or it is a lie.

Cold-start p95 — not measured yet. You asked for the number and said nothing waits on it. It needs an instrumented build on the Galaxy, so it comes with the device-QA pass, not from here.

One row I had to rebuild, and why it matters to you. The “No watch needed” row first asked how often the line appears — on every beat, or at the CTA only. Driving all 39 option/structure combinations through the assembler showed those two options producing a byte-identical frame on both single-screen structures: with one screen, “every” and “the last one” are the same screen. That is a dead option, and you would have been picking between two things that render the same. It is now a placement decision — under the headline, at the CTA, or not on this screen — which stays distinct in all three structures. All 39 combinations now change the frame.

Fonts. System faces, because this file runs offline. The real screen uses General Sans for headlines; tracking and letterform will differ, proportions won't.
