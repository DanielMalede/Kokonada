Correcting your framing on the Sleep bar — because it changes the fix

You said the Sleep bar reads as a different hue family from the two bars above it. It isn’t a different hue. Variability and Resting heart rate are single fills at var(--accent). Sleep is the same accent, drawn as three stacked segments at opacity 1, .55 and .5. So the difference is grammar, not colour — one bar means “how much of a range”, the other means “what this is made of”, and they are drawn as if they meant the same thing.

That matters because re-tinting would not fix it. Unifying the grammar would. Row one is that decision, and it has a third option that follows the code: GAUGES lists Deep sleep and REM sleep as two separate gauges. There is no combined “Sleep” gauge in the shipped presentation layer at all.

Restoring the two-part structure — a short label with a quieter second line — is the whole fix, and it is what the code was already describing. §8 also has a shorter variant for the no-read case: “Not shared by your watch.”

One rule no option here will break

pulsePresentation.ts is explicit about why every gauge fill is the same colour:

“Display-normalization ranges — NOT medical thresholds. They only map a value to a fill magnitude 0..1; the fill’s colour is fixed, so a low or high number is never coloured ‘bad’ or ‘good’. Number carries meaning; the capsule only carries a proportion.”

So no option below tints a gauge by its value, and none should be added later. On a screen showing someone their own body, colouring a number as bad is the difference between a dashboard and a diagnosis. Worth also noting Readiness is still on this screen — that is the dailyReadiness consent item, and it is displayed today.
