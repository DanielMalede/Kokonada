### REG-226 · FDA: no reference to disease, clinical thresholds or clinical equivalence
- kind: PROHIBITION
- rule: No labeling, listing, website or in-app text:
  - refers to a disease, a clinical condition or a diagnostic threshold;
  - claims clinical equivalence or "medical grade";
  - prompts any clinical action.
  Wellness claims stay inside the §520(o)(1)(B) wording.
- source:
  - FD&C Act §520(o)(1)(B), as quoted by the FDA, excludes software "for maintaining or encouraging a healthy lifestyle and is unrelated to the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment of a disease or condition". https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/digital-health-center-excellence/step-3-software-function-intended-maintaining-or-encouraging-healthy-lifestyle [RETRIEVED; content current as of 27 Sep 2022]
  - 21 CFR 801.4: intended use "may, for example, be shown by labeling claims, advertising matter, or oral or written statements by such persons or their representatives" (86 FR 41401). eCFR API [RETRIEVED]
  - General Wellness guidance, reissued January 2026 (docket FDA-2014-N-1039): the landing page is RETRIEVED; the text is UNRETRIEVED (§9). Commentary [COM] reports a ban on values that "mimic those used clinically" and on "References to specific diseases, clinical conditions, or diagnostic thresholds".
