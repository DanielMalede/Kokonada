### j2-brief-compliance.md · verdict on the plan · lines 5–10

**Verdict on the plan: NEEDS CHANGE. No HALT on building J2. HALT-B stays in force on the store filings.**

The plan fixes the mechanism behind HALT-B well: the purge, the start gate, the re-check with undo, writing a withdrawal row first on deletion (M1′), the fixed failure reason, and a job id that is a keyed pseudonym. As written, though, it does not close HALT-B for Google Play or Apple, for three reasons:
1. **It declares an unbounded leftover.** If the server dies between a job's write and its re-check, the profile stays with "no retention limit of its own". Leaving BE-094 out of J2 (S0) means the nightly analysis keeps that profile alive.
2. **The event-log cap is a count, not a time.** The pseudonym in the queue's event log is bounded only by entry count. Play counts re-linkable pseudonymous data as user data.
3. **The batch-ingest race still re-creates the same document** (BE-028), and becomes the main remaining path once J2 lands.

### j2-brief-regulatory.md · §1, Q6, is there an Art. 7 issue with M1′'s withdrawal row when a deletion fails? · lines 131–147

**Facts:**
- **The row cannot say who wrote it.** M1′ appends a row of the same form as the user's own withdrawal (`consent.js:106-112`). The schema allows only `granted` or `withdrawn` and has no field saying who or what wrote the row (`ConsentRecord.js:30-36`).
- **On success,** the cascade erases the row with the rest of the history (`erasure.js:80`).
- **On failure,** three things follow:
  - the account remains and reads as withdrawn;
  - the export shows the row;
  - the withdrawal's own erasure steps do not run.
- **The plan's claim needs a qualifier.** "On failure, the history stays truthful" holds only if `ConsentRecord.deleteMany` did not complete. It runs in the same `Promise.all` as the health deletes (`erasure.js:52-81`). So health data can be left with no consent history at all, which is M1's gap. That gap exists today, and it remains under M1′.

**Readings:**
- **Stopping is not the problem.** Art. 7(3) is the data subject's right to withdraw. It neither requires nor forbids the controller ceasing to rely on consent. EDPB 05/2020 para 117 says that on withdrawal "the controller must stop the processing actions concerned". I found no text that makes stopping unlawful.
- **Accuracy and proof (Art. 5(1)(d), 7(1), 5(2)) are the open points.** EDPB 05/2020 para 108 frames the record as showing "how consent was obtained, when consent was obtained". A row the deletion wrote is attributed to the user. Whether that is "inaccurate, having regard to the purposes" is counsel's question. A field saying where the row came from would remove the ambiguity; adding one is a design choice.
- **Transparency.** A user whose deletion failed sees a withdrawal they did not make. Text (g) declares it.

**For counsel:** J2-Q5.
