### The architect · v1b-B4-architect-admission.md · §1 item 4, progress and bounds, slots · lines 36–46

> 4. **Admission is the arrival-order prefix, stopping at the first change that does not fit.** The limits are judged on the prefix's net change against what is on screen:
>    - at most 2 entering and at most 2 leaving (this is how "two bead swaps" becomes something to compute);
>    - at most 3 live afterwards;
>    - live + entering + leaving + rest lobes (fade > 0 or rising) ≤ `NODE_SLOTS`.

> **Progress and bounds:**
> - A single bead event always fits (5 nodes or fewer), so every crossfade admits at least one event.
> - After cancellation, the net change is at most 3 leaving and 3 entering, which takes 2 crossfades. That gives resilience's "latest placements within 1.8 s of the last tap": up to 600 ms waiting, plus 600 ms, plus a 600 ms ramp.
> - The designer's planted case (Linen, three neutrals, two taps) comes out as 6 nodes, then 7, so it is admitted as one swap and then one swap.
>
> **Slots:** each node keeps its slot from entering to leaving, and freed slots are reused lowest first. The allocator lives in the ledger. Drift stays keyed by ordinal.

### The resilience-auditor · v1b-B4-resilience-admission.md · the follow-up verdict and §1 · lines 1, 3–5 and 10–30 (handed back 2026-10-06T11:43:27Z)

> # Resilience: admission, bead layer, CTA counter (verbatim, handed back 2026-10-06T11:43:27.328Z)

> ## Follow-up verdict
> - **Admission without eviction: ACCEPT.** It removes the last source of a fade step. Three corrections are needed so E3 does not pass falsely; they follow.
> - **Unserialized bead layer: ACCEPT.** The area argument holds.

> ### 1. Does admission keep E3's bounds?
> - **No fade moves faster than its curve: yes.** Starts are serialized, so every fade is 0 or 1 when a crossfade begins, and nothing is ever cut.
> - **Starts at least 600 ms apart: yes**, unchanged. A single swap always fits (at most 3 + 1 + 2 = 6 nodes), so a crossfade never stalls. Pin this as "every start admits at least one pending change".
> - **"packFrame never throws" proves nothing as the builder stands.**
>   - `fieldFrame.ts:155` (`slice(-3)`) and `:185` (`restLobes.slice(0, NODE_SLOTS - nodes.length)`) quietly drop nodes.
>   - At 6 nodes, both rest lobes are dropped even when lit; at 5 nodes, one is dropped. That is a cut.
>   - Above 6 nodes, the negative end in `slice(0, -k)` pushes extra lobes, so `packFrame` throws. Below that, overflow shows up as a cut, not as a throw.
>   - So the demand must be counted from the ledger's output, not the built frame, and the builder must throw instead of slicing.
> - **The 1.8 s convergence bound is false.**
>   - **The Linen case:** three neutral placements with lit rest lobes admit only one swap per crossfade (3 + 1 + 2 = 6; two swaps would need 7). Replacing all three takes 3 crossfades.
>   - The next start can come up to 600 ms after the last tap, so convergence is at most 600·k + ramp.
>   - With k = 3, that is 2.4 s in settle and 1.95 s under reduced motion. It is 1.8 s / 1.35 s only where no rest lobe is lit (k ≤ 2).
>   - **The 2.4 s bound also requires netting:** pending changes must reduce to the difference between the field's current set and the store's latest set. A raw first-in-first-out queue admitting 2 changes per 600 ms lags about 0.3 s per tap, without limit (a 7 taps/s, 10 s storm is about 21 s behind).
>
> **Text change, in "Designer 2.6, amended":**
> - Old: "The field shows the latest placements within 1.8 s of the last tap."
> - New: "Pending changes are netted against the store's latest placements, never queued raw. The field shows the latest placements within 2.4 s of the last tap (1.95 s under reduced motion), and within 1.8 s (1.35 s) wherever no rest lobe is lit."
>
> **Text change, in the designer's added bullet:**
> - Old: "a halo may trail its bead by up to one crossfade interval"
> - New: "a halo may trail its bead until its change is admitted: at most 2.4 s after the last tap (1.95 s under reduced motion)"
