### The scribe's notes

- what this keeps: the controller on the privacy page stays the owner as a natural person in Israel, with no postal address (DEC-147). "Kokonada" is the name of the product and the trade name; it is not a company. No document, form or page may call it a company, an "Inc." or a "Ltd", or imply a registered entity.
- the risk the owner takes with this answer (not a ruling, a fact to weigh): Garmin's Starter access is described on its page as "the starting point for organizations building apps" and its form opens with the program being meant for businesses, the submitter being the party that owns the app. Both variants of the Garmin Connect Developer Program Agreement let "you" be the Licensee when no entity is named (the preamble of FRM-0952 Rev. B, International and RHQ), so the contract itself does not require an entity; the form's own eligibility text decides whether the application is accepted, and that text is Garmin's to apply. If Garmin declines for lack of an entity, the fallback is DEC-044 (an entity, with a D-U-N-S number if Garmin or Play ask for one), and the application is made again.
- the form's address fields: the form asks for an address, city, country and postal code. They go to Garmin only, in the form, and the owner supplies them. DEC-147's "no postal address" is about the published privacy page and does not change.
- what the application text must say, so that it is true: that the product is built and owned by one independent developer and that no legal entity exists yet (the business-model text of the application, TASKS `DAN-103`).
