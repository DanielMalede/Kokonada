### The scribe's notes

None of these notes is a ruling; each is the scribe's.

- what this keeps: the controller on the privacy page stays the owner as a natural person in Israel, with no postal address (DEC-147). DEC-147's "no postal address" is not a statement that the page meets GDPR Art. 13 or Israel's Privacy Protection Law s.11; that is counsel's question (`DAN-014`).
- the scribe's reading of the name, not a ruling: "Kokonada" is the name of the product; whether it is a trade name is not ruled. The scribe's recommendation, not a ruling: no document, form or page calls it a company, an "Inc." or a "Ltd", or implies a registered entity, since the owner's words say there is no legal entity.
- the risk the owner takes with this answer (a fact to weigh): Garmin's overview page (`developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/overview/`, read 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-27) describes Starter access as "the starting point for organizations building apps", and the same page speaks of applications "from individual developers and startups". Garmin's FAQ, as read on 2026-09-29, said the program "is only for business use"; that FAQ address now returns 404 and the current FAQ has no such sentence (`OPS-334`), while the overview page addresses "organizations" and names "individual developers and startups". The Starter form's own opening and eligibility text is UNVERIFIED: the form was never read (HTTP 522 on 2026-10-25, and it is rendered by JavaScript), so what it says about businesses comes from secondary sources only. Both variants of the Garmin Connect Developer Program Agreement let "you" be the Licensee when no entity is named (the preamble of FRM-0952 Rev. B, International and RHQ); that the contract itself therefore does not require an entity is the scribe's reading, for counsel or Garmin to confirm, and the "organizations" wording may still be read against an individual. Whether the application is accepted is Garmin's to decide. If Garmin declines for lack of an entity, DEC-044 is put to the owner again, and the application is made again on his answer.
- the form's address fields: UNVERIFIED, since the form was never read; the owner's list and the secondary sources say it asks for an address, city, country and postal code. If it does, they go to Garmin only, in the form, and the owner supplies them. DEC-147's "no postal address" is about the published privacy page and does not change.
- what the application text must say, so that it is true: that the product is built and owned by one independent developer and that no legal entity exists yet (the business-model text of the application, TASKS `DAN-103`).
